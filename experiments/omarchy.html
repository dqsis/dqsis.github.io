Omarchy.
My Desktop.

My laptop runs Omarchy, an opinionated Linux setup built on Arch and the Hyprland tiling window manager. It comes ready to use, but I can't stop tuning it.

What is Omarchy →

What it is

Omarchy takes a bare Arch Linux install and turns it into a full, keyboard-driven desktop: windows tile themselves, everything opens from a launcher, and one command switches the theme across the bar, terminal, editor and wallpaper. The defaults are strong. My own config lives in a few small override files that load on top of them, so updates keep improving the base without overwriting my changes.

My tweaks

  • Idle chain: screensaver after 10 minutes, lock after 45, suspend after 60. The first two come from my own clone of Omarchy's idle service, dqsis.idle, which launches my screensaver instead of the default. A small Quickshell plugin I wrote, dqsis.idle-suspend, adds the suspend step. Both respect idle inhibitors and the Stay Awake toggle.
  • Screensaver: a fullscreen terminal on every screen running asciiquarium on pure black, with the cursor hidden (cmatrix is one line away). Any key or a change of focus closes it.
  • Bar layout: menu and workspaces on the left; the clock in the centre, flanked by indicators, keyboard layout, weather and the update check; tray (with NordVPN pinned), agents, Bluetooth, network, audio, monitor and power on the right.
  • Clock: shows the weekday, date and time (dddd d MMMM HH:mm). Its alternate view shows the date with the week number (d MMMM 'W'ww yyyy), and the calendar popup includes a life-progress bar.
  • Keyboard: Swedish and Greek layouts, switched with Right Ctrl. Caps Lock is a Compose key; pressing both Shifts toggles real Caps Lock.
  • Two screens: the laptop panel at 1.25× scale and a 34-inch 3440×1440 ultrawide over HDMI, run at 30 Hz.
