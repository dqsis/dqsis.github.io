The Optimist and the Pessimist

The optimist proclaims that we live in the best of all possible worlds; and the pessimist fears this is true.

James Branch Cabell, The Silver Stallion: A Comedy of Redemption (1926) — Book Four, ch. XXVI, “The Realist in Defeat,” spoken by the character Coth. Again by way of Έρωτας και άλλες πολεμικές τέχνες.
Note

The optimist and the pessimist say the same thing — this is the best of all possible worlds — but one proclaims it and the other fears it is true. They don’t disagree about the world; they just feel differently about it.

(The line is often credited to Oppenheimer, who quoted a looser version. The original is Cabell’s, twenty years earlier.)
